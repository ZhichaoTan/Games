# Games

Table games for phone browsers that you play online with friends. There's no sign-up: pick a name, open a table and send the four-letter room code (or the invite link) to your friends.

- **Home page**, with the list of games: https://zhichaotan.github.io/Games/
- **Midnight Hold'em**: https://zhichaotan.github.io/Games/poker/
- **Sichuan Mahjong**: https://zhichaotan.github.io/Games/mahjong/
- **Xiangqi** (Chinese chess): https://zhichaotan.github.io/Games/xiangqi/
- **Cipher** (number tiles): https://zhichaotan.github.io/Games/cipher/
- **Moontide** (island escape): https://zhichaotan.github.io/Games/moontide/
- **Shiba Stack** (falling blocks): https://zhichaotan.github.io/Games/shiba/
- **Puffy Pals** (match three): https://zhichaotan.github.io/Games/match/

Tap a game on the home page to open its lobby, and the "All games" button at the top of each lobby goes back. Each game has its own rooms, so friends can be in different games at the same time. Your name, avatar and language carry over from game to game. Everything is in English by default, and the globe button switches to Chinese.

Hold'em used to be at the top of the site, so an old invite link like `/Games/#ABCD` still opens that Hold'em room.

## How online play works

The host's browser runs the table. Friends connect to it directly over WebRTC (PeerJS for the handshake, public STUN/TURN servers for the connection), so there's no game server and nothing is stored online. The host keeps the page open while you play.

- If someone's connection drops, a bot plays their turns until they come back. Opening the same link again puts them back in their seat.
- Friends who arrive after the game has started take over a bot's seat.
- The host can add or remove bots, change their level, remove players, and save the game to carry on later with the same room code.
- Short of players? Add bots, or practice on your own against bots. Practice works offline.

## Midnight Hold'em

No-limit Texas Hold'em for 2 to 9 players.

- Bots come in four levels: IQ 70 (Rookie), 100 (Regular), 130 (Shark) and 160 (Genius). Pick one level or a mix, and the host can tap a bot's IQ in the menu to change it. Bots that go broke buy back in by themselves. Bot speed (Instant, Fast, Normal, Slow) is set under Practice or in the host's menu.
- Points: 1 point buys 1 chip. Everyone gets 5,000 free points a day. Sitting down takes your buy-in from your points, and leaving turns your chips back into points. Three mini-games earn more points (Higher or Lower, Who Wins?, Memory Pairs), and there's a switch for unlimited points. Points are kept in this browser only.
- Odds helper (the % button): your chance of winning if the other players still in hold random cards, the win chance a call needs to pay off, and your chances of making each hand by the river. It's off by default.
- The host can lock the room.
- Need a break? The host's Leave button offers "Save & continue later". The hand being played is called off, everyone's chips and what they've borrowed are kept, and the same room code brings everyone back. "End the game" shows the final standings and turns everyone's chips back into points.
- Going broke: borrow another stack, from your points or free with unlimited points. What each player has borrowed shows in front of their chips, and the player list shows each player's win or loss.
- Fair seats: cards are shuffled with the browser's cryptographic random numbers (Fisher–Yates), the first dealer is drawn at random, and the button moves one seat every hand. Large simulations of the engine show the same card luck, all-in win rate and blind costs for every seat.

## Sichuan Mahjong

Four players, Bloody Battle (血战到底) rules, every player for themselves or in 2v2 teams.

- 108 tiles: Characters, Bamboo and Dots, 1 to 9, four of each. There are no chows; you can only pung, kong or win.
- Swap three: after the deal everyone passes three tiles of one suit, clockwise, counter-clockwise or across. The host can turn this off.
- Void suit: everyone gives up one suit. You can't win while you hold any of it and you can't pung or kong it, but when you discard it is up to you (the host can make it go first). Anyone still holding some when the wall runs out is a flower pig.
- Bloody Battle: whoever wins steps out and the others play on, until one player is left or the wall runs out. Several players can win on the same discard.
- Passing a win: if you could win on someone's tile and don't, then until your next draw you can only win on a discard worth more fan than the one you passed. The table says so while it applies.
- Kongs pay at once: an exposed kong is paid by the player who discarded the tile, the others by everyone still playing. If the tile you discard right after a kong lets someone win, that kong's money goes to the winner.
- When the wall runs out, a player still holding their void suit pays everyone, players who aren't ready pay those who are, and players who aren't ready hand back their kong money.
- A win scores the base points × 2 to the power of the fan count, up to the room's fan cap. Fan come from the hand (All Pungs, Full Flush, Seven Pairs, Golden Hook, 2-5-8 Pungs, Terminals Everywhere and more), from each set of four identical tiles, and from how you won (Kong Bloom, Robbing the Kong, Last Tile, Heavenly and Earthly Hands). A self-drawn win collects from everyone still playing, plus one base point or one fan, as the host sets.
- Dealer: the first dealer is random. The first player to win deals the next round, or the discarder if that first win was several players winning on one discard. If nobody wins, the dealer stays.
- Host options: free-for-all or 2v2 teams, number of rounds, fan cap, how self-draws score, how a win off a discarded kong counts, swap three, whether the void suit must be discarded first, extra fan (terminals and 2-5-8, concealed and all simples, heavenly and earthly hands), base points and a turn timer.
- 2v2 teams: partners sit across from each other and play as one side. You see your partner's hand face up and they see yours, while the other team sees neither. You can't win off your partner's discard, money only moves between the two teams, and the round ends once both players of one team have won. In the room, the host taps "Make partner" on the player they want to team up with, and the other two play together. In practice your partner is the bot across the table.
- Partner swap: on your turn, after you draw, tap Swap, pick the tile you want from your partner's hand and one of yours to give back (of a different kind), and tap Ask. Your partner sees what the swap does for both hands and taps Swap or No; after 20 seconds it counts as a no. A swap you get counts as a fresh draw, so you can win on the tile your partner gave you. One request per turn, and swaps stop once either partner has won. The host sets how many swaps each player gets per round: 1, 2, 3 (the default), 5 or no limit, and your name plate and your partner's always show how many each of you has left. Bots ask for a swap when it wins them the hand, or when it helps without setting either hand back, and they agree to swaps that cost them little.
- Bots come in four levels: Rookie, Regular, Shark and Genius. Stronger bots pick better swaps and void suits, count the tiles that have gone and play safe when someone looks ready.
- Tap a tile to select it, then tap it again or drag it up to discard it. You can also slide along your hand to pick a tile. With hints on, a dot marks the tiles that leave you ready, and a selected tile shows what you'd be waiting on and how many of those tiles are left.
- Autoplay hands your turns to a bot for a while, and "Take over" gives them back.

## Xiangqi

Chinese chess for two: online with a friend, on one phone passed back and forth, or against the computer.

- Online: the host opens a room and sends the four-letter code or the invite link. The host picks who plays Red (or lets it be random) and an optional clock of 10, 20 or 30 minutes each; the sides swap after every game and the room keeps the score. Taking a move back and offering a draw need the other player's agreement. A clock only runs while both players are connected. If either player's page reloads or their connection drops, opening the room again carries on from the same position.
- Against the computer: seven levels, from a beginner that drops pieces to one that thinks for a few seconds a move. You choose Red, Black or random, can take moves back, ask for a hint, and resume an unfinished game later.
- Rules: stalemate is a loss for the side that cannot move, the generals may not face each other, perpetual check loses, a position that keeps repeating without check is a draw, and so are sixty moves without a capture or a position where neither side has an attacking piece left.
- Explore: at any point you can try moves out on the board for both sides without touching the real game, then go back to it.
- Review: every finished game can be replayed with a chart of Red's winning chances, each move graded (best, good, inaccuracy, mistake, blunder), the engine's best move with the line behind it, and a report with each side's accuracy and key mistakes. From any position in a review you can explore with the engine's candidate moves, or play on from there against the computer.
- Two engines. The built-in one is part of the page and is ready at once. [Pikafish](https://github.com/official-pikafish/Pikafish), a much stronger engine, is optional: switching it on downloads 48 MB once and keeps it in the browser. It can be the opponent, and it makes hints and reviews far more accurate.
- In Chinese the pieces carry their characters and moves are written the traditional way (炮二平五). In English the pieces are figurines and moves use WXF notation (C2=5). Settings can switch the pieces either way, and choose a dark or a wooden board.
- Results and game records are kept in this browser. A save code (Lobby, "Save code") turns them into a piece of text to move to another phone or browser. Save codes from the earlier stand-alone version of this game work too.

## Cipher

A deduction game for 2 to 4 players with black and white number tiles, played by the rules of the tile game also known as Coda or Da Vinci Code. In Chinese it is called 数字密码. The tiles, the key and the lettering are drawn for this site.

- 24 tiles, 0 to 11 in black and 0 to 11 in white, plus two keys. Everyone sees the colour of every tile; only its owner sees the number. Each row stands from smallest to largest, and black goes left of white on the same number.
- With two or three players everyone gets 4 tiles, with four players 3. On your turn you draw a black or a white tile, then pick an opponent's hidden tile and name its number. Right, and it turns face up: guess again or stop, and your new tile joins your row face down. Wrong, and your new tile goes into your row face up. When the pool is empty you guess without drawing, and a wrong guess turns up one of your own tiles.
- Keys have no number and can stand anywhere in a row; whoever draws one chooses where. A hidden tile can be guessed as the key. The host can play without keys.
- A player whose tiles are all face up is out, and the last player with a hidden tile wins the round. Each round scores a point for every player you outlast, and the next round starts one seat on.
- Wrong guesses stay noted under the tile for everyone to see. Hints on the number pad are off by default, since working the numbers out is the game. Settings can switch them to simple (greys out what the row order, the tiles you can see and earlier wrong guesses rule out) or exact (works through the whole table, including every wrong guess, and shows each number's chance).
- Bots come in four levels: Rookie, Regular, Shark and Genius. Rookie reasons loosely, Regular works out the table and plays safe, Shark also presses on when the odds are good, and Genius looks one guess ahead. Head to head over a few thousand bot-only rounds, each level beats the one below it: Regular wins about three rounds in four against Rookie, Shark nearly two in three against Regular, and Genius about 54% against Shark.
- Online rooms hold four seats and empty seats can take bots. The host sets keys and a turn timer (30, 60 or 90 seconds, 60 by default, or none); a player who runs out of time gets a bot's move, and two timeouts in a row hand their turns to a bot until they tap "Take over". Friends who arrive mid-round take over a bot's seat or join from the next round.
- Practice needs no connection: play 1 to 3 bots of one level or a mix, and an unfinished practice game can be resumed from the lobby.

## Moontide

A race off a sinking island for 2 to 4 players. In Chinese it is called 沉岛逃生. The rules, the board and the artwork are this site's own.

- The board is a hex sea with a lighthouse on each of its six corners and an island of 24, 30 or 32 tiles (for 2, 3 or 4 players, so everyone gets the same number of turns) in the middle: beaches, palm groves and hills. Everyone has the same set of islanders, each carrying 1 to 5 pearls, and only you can see which of yours carries what.
- Setting up: players take turns putting one islander at a time on an empty tile (not the crab's hill), then put a boat each in the water beside the island, in the same order. "Quick start" deals everything out at random instead.
- A turn: the tide die is rolled first (shark, shark, octopus, octopus, crab, or the moon for any creature). Then you take 3 steps, sink one tile, and may move one creature of the kind you rolled.
- Steps: an islander walks to a neighbouring tile (three to a tile at most, never onto the crab), climbs into a boat with room, or jumps into the sea and swims from then on, one hex a turn, never into a shark. A boat holds three and moves one hex a step; whoever has the most people aboard rows it, and anyone may row an empty one. Sailing into a lighthouse saves everyone aboard, and the empty boat can be rowed back out for more. Undo takes a step back.
- The tide: only tiles on the shore can sink. At low tide that means beaches, after a third of the island has gone palm groves too, and after two thirds anything. People on a sinking tile end up in the water, and the tile is turned over: a boat (anyone who just fell in climbs aboard), a shark or an octopus, a reef (sharks and octopuses can't cross it, and boats can't either), a dolphin (carry any swimmer up to three hexes, into a lighthouse too), a lifebuoy or a tailwind to keep for later (the buoy saves all your swimmers from one shark, the tailwind adds two steps to a turn), or nothing.
- Creatures: a shark swims up to three hexes and eats every swimmer where it stops; an octopus stays put or moves one hex and can drag a boat in or next to its hex under, leaving everyone aboard swimming; the crab walks up to two tiles over the island and shoves anyone on the tile it walks onto into the water beside it.
- The game ends when the last tile goes under: everyone not in a lighthouse is lost. The most pearls in the lighthouses wins, then the most people saved; a tie after that is a shared win.
- Bots come in three levels (Easy, Normal and Hard) or a mix. A bot sees only what a player sees: its own pearls and nobody else's. Over bot-only matches Hard beats Easy almost every time head to head and wins most games at a table with three Easy bots.
- Online rooms hold four seats and empty seats can take bots. The host sets the setup and a turn timer (45 or 90 seconds, or none, the default); a player who runs out of time gets a bot's move, and two timeouts in a row hand their turns to a bot until they tap "Take over". Friends who arrive mid-game take over a bot's seat. Bots' names show in each reader's language.
- Practice needs no connection: play 1 to 3 bots at the speed you like, and an unfinished practice game can be resumed from the lobby.

## Shiba Stack

Falling blocks for 1 to 4 players, on your own or in online battles. In Chinese it is called 快乐柴柴. The look, the shiba and the names are this site's own; the block rules are the ones most modern falling-block games use.

- The well is 10 cells wide and 20 high. The seven blocks come from a shuffled bag of one of each, so no block is ever long in coming. Each block has a shiba face with ears. You can hold one block for later (once per block), the dashed outline shows where it will land, and the next five are shown. Turning uses wall kicks, and a block can still slide or turn for half a second after it lands (up to 15 times).
- Points: one to four rows at once score 100, 300, 500 or 800 times the level. A T-Twist (turning the T block into a slot it couldn't drop into) scores more, clears in a row add a combo bonus, and two big clears in a row (four rows or T-Twists) score half as much again. Emptying the whole well (an all clear) scores extra.
- Solo: Marathon gets faster every 10 rows (start at level 1, 5, 10 or 15), Sprint 40 is a race to clear 40 rows, Two Minutes is for points, and vs Bots is a battle against 1 to 3 bots. Records are kept in this browser, and an unfinished game can be picked up again from the lobby.
- Battles: only a clear of three rows or more at once sends bubble rows to an opponent. Three rows send 2, four send 4 and a T-Twist triple 6, a back-to-back clear sends one more, and if the clear also empties the well (an all clear) it sends 10. Unlike most falling-block games, one or two rows send nothing, so small quick clears can't bury anyone. Bubble rows wait 0.6 seconds beside your well, then rise from the bottom the next time you place a block without clearing (8 at a time at most). A clear that sends rows cancels waiting rows first. With more than one opponent your rows go to a random one, or tap a board to aim at that player. The speed goes up every 30 seconds, a knock-out counts for whoever sent that player rows in the 15 seconds before, and the last player standing wins the round.
- Online rooms hold four seats, and empty seats can take bots. Everyone plays their own well and the host's page starts each round with the same blocks for all. A player whose page reloads, or who is gone for 8 seconds in the middle of a round, is out of that round and plays again from the next. Friends who arrive during a round join from the next one. The host can change the bots' level, remove players and go back to the room between rounds, and the room counts each seat's wins.
- Controls: on phones, swipe (drag sideways to move, drag down to drop faster, flick down to drop at once, flick up to hold, tap to turn) or buttons at the bottom; on a keyboard, arrows, Space, Z and X, C or Shift to hold, Esc to pause. The menu sets the swipe speed, the key repeat and the landing outline. Solo games pause when the page goes to the background.
- Bots come in four levels: Rookie, Regular, Pro and Master. They play by the same rules and take a while over each block (Rookie 3 seconds, Regular 2, Pro 1.5, Master 1.1): they decide, move the block into place, and drop it when the time is up. When a block lands before they are done (late in a battle, when blocks fall fast) they play more carelessly, as people do. Rookie cashes in early with three-row clears, the others build higher for four-row clears, Master looks one block ahead, and the lower the level the more often a block is dropped carelessly. In bot-only battles each level beats the one below (Regular beats Rookie in 11 of 12 battles, Pro beats Regular in 9 of 12, Master beats Pro in 12 of 12). Against person-like players played by the same code (a casual one takes about 2 seconds a block and slips now and then; these settings are guesses, not measurements), the casual player beats Rookie about 3 times in 4, Regular a bit under half the time and Pro 1 time in 4, and only a practised player takes games off Master (about 1 in 3).
- The sounds and the music are made in the page. The music speeds up with the level.

## Puffy Pals

A match-three game for 1 to 4 players, on your own or in online races. In Chinese it is called 萌宠消消乐. The six animals (a piglet, a penguin, a chick, a lamb, a fox and a frog), the levels and the artwork are this site's own. A second look, Mochi Moon (团团圆圆), swaps the animals for round mochi at a lantern fair: the Theme row in the lobby switches between the two, and the choice is kept.

- Swapping: drag an animal into the next cell, or tap it and then its neighbour. A swap counts if it lines up three or more of a kind or makes a 2×2 square; otherwise the two go back. Matched animals pop, the ones above drop, and new ones fall in from the top. Anything that lines up as they land pops too, and each wave of a chain scores half as much again. A board with no move left is shuffled.
- Specials: four in a line make a rocket that clears its row or column, an L or T shape a firecracker that blasts the cells around it, a 2×2 square a sky lantern that pops its neighbours and then flies to a cell that helps your goal, and five in a line a firework: swap it with an animal and every animal of that kind goes. Tap a special or swap it to set it off. Two specials swapped together make a bigger blast, up to two fireworks clearing the whole board.
- Levels: sixty levels in six places on a map, opened in order. Each has goals (points, animals to collect, frost to clear, bamboo cages to break, gift boxes to open, gold ingots to bring down) and a number of moves. Moves left over become rockets for extra points, the score gives one to three stars, and each level has one hammer that smashes any cell without using a move. Most boards are 8×8; some are 7×7 or 9×9, and some have cells cut out. The first level with each special or obstacle shows how it works. Progress is kept in this browser, and an unfinished level can be picked up again from the lobby.
- Time Rush is 90 seconds for points, with your best kept. vs Bots is the same race against 1 to 3 bots, all on the same board.
- Online rooms hold four seats, and empty seats can take bots. Everyone starts from the same board, and the host picks how long a round lasts (60, 90 or 120 seconds). Each player sees the others' boards small, with their scores, and can send quick phrases and emoji. A chain of three waves or more, a firework or two specials together drop bamboo cages on whoever is in the lead among the others (at most 4 per move; the host can turn cages off). The highest score wins the round, and the room counts each seat's wins. A guest whose page reloads during a round comes back to the same seat with the same board. Friends who arrive during a round join from the next one. The host can change the bots' level, remove players and go back to the room between rounds.
- Bots come in four levels: Rookie, Regular, Pro and Master. They play by the same rules, wait for each move to play out like a person, and take a while to choose (about 3.3, 2.5, 1.9 and 1.3 seconds); the lower levels see fewer of the possible moves, choose less well and care less about making specials. Over the same 24 boards of 90 seconds they average about 5,900, 8,200, 11,700 and 14,500 points. Person-like players played by the same code (guesses, not measurements) land between them: a beginner about 6,500, a casual player about 8,800 and a practised one about 12,600. In 60 four-bot battles with cages, Master won 45 and Pro 13. A casual player against three Regular bots wins about 1 round in 5 (an even share would be 1 in 4).
- Levels were tuned the same way: a simulated casual player clears about 96% of the boards in the first ten levels and about two in three in the last ten.
- Controls work by touch or mouse. The menu has sound effects, music, vibration, hints after a pause and quicker animations. The sounds and the music are made in the page. Solo games pause when the page goes to the background.

## Build

Each page is one self-contained HTML file that GitHub Pages serves as it is. The sources are next to it, and each build script inlines the fonts, artwork and code.

- Home page: the source is `hub/index.html`. `python3 hub/build.py` writes the root `index.html`. It reuses the fonts, avatars and seal from `poker/src/`, four tile faces from `mahjong/src/tiles.js`, the piece font and three figurines from `xiangqi/src/`, and the key, the keyhole and the card pattern from `cipher/src/art.js`, the lobby pictures from `moontide/src/art.js` and `shiba/src/art.js`, and the Puffy Pals card picture from `match/src/art.js` and `match/src/critters.js`.
- Hold'em: the source is in `poker/src/`. `python3 poker/src/build.py` writes `poker/index.html`.
- Mahjong: the source is in `mahjong/src/`. `python3 mahjong/src/build.py` writes `mahjong/index.html`. It reuses the fonts, PeerJS, avatars and the zoom guard (`nozoom.js`) from `poker/src/`.
- Xiangqi: the source is in `xiangqi/src/`. `python3 xiangqi/src/build.py` writes `xiangqi/index.html`. The app is `src/app/*.js`, joined in file-name order inside one function. It reuses the fonts, PeerJS, avatars and the zoom guard from `poker/src/`. The Pikafish engine in `xiangqi/pf/` is not inlined: the page fetches it when a player switches it on (`xiangqi/pf/README.md` says how it was built).
- Cipher: the source is in `cipher/src/`. `python3 cipher/src/build.py` writes `cipher/index.html`. The app is `src/app/*.js`, joined in file-name order inside one function. It reuses the fonts, PeerJS, avatars, the zoom guard and the room keep-alive (`keepalive.js`) from `poker/src/`, and the table sounds from `mahjong/src/sfx.js`.
- Moontide: the source is in `moontide/src/`. `python3 moontide/src/build.py` writes `moontide/index.html`. The app is `src/app/*.js`, joined in file-name order inside one function. It reuses the fonts, PeerJS, avatars, the zoom guard and the room keep-alive (`keepalive.js`) from `poker/src/`; its sounds are made in the page (`src/sound.js`).
- Puffy Pals: the source is in `match/src/`. `python3 match/src/build.py` writes `match/index.html`. The app is `src/app/*.js`, joined in file-name order inside one function. It reuses the fonts, PeerJS, avatars, the zoom guard and the room keep-alive (`keepalive.js`) from `poker/src/`; the animals are drawn in `src/critters.js` and everything else in `src/art.js`, and its sounds and music are made in the page (`src/sound.js`).
- Puffy Pals tests: `node match/tests/engine.test.js` checks the rules on hand-made boards (matching, every special and every pair of specials, chains, the hammer, frost, cages, gift boxes and ingots, winning and losing a level, stars, the clock, saving, cages thrown in a battle, the small boards sent to other players, shuffling) and that every level builds and plays to the end. `node match/tests/sim.js` plays every level with a simulated casual player, 90-second games with every bot level and three person-like players, and four-bot battles with and without cages, and fails if the levels or the bots fall out of line. `node match/dev/i18n-check.js` checks that every string exists in both languages and both looks, and that the two display fonts have every character they draw. `match/dev/` has the Playwright scripts: `smoke.js` (lobby screenshots in both looks and languages, a level, Time Rush and a battle), `layout.js` (the game at nine screen sizes, a level and a battle against three bots, checking that nothing runs off the screen or overlaps), `sheets.js` (the theme picker, the menu, the rules and every result card) and `online.js` (three browsers and a bot on a local PeerServer: invite link and room code, round time, rounds played with real moves, chat, cages, a guest reloading mid-round and keeping their board, results, the next round, back to the room, removing a guest and the host closing the room), plus `balance.js` (sets each level's moves and star scores), `tutseeds.js` (picks the boards that teach each special) and `makefont.py` (the two display-font subsets).
- Shiba Stack: the source is in `shiba/src/`. `python3 shiba/src/build.py` writes `shiba/index.html`. The app is `src/app/*.js`, joined in file-name order inside one function. It reuses the fonts, PeerJS, avatars, the zoom guard and the room keep-alive (`keepalive.js`) from `poker/src/`; its sounds and music are made in the page (`src/sound.js`).
- Shiba Stack tests: `node shiba/tests/engine.test.js` checks the rules on hand-made wells (the bag, moving, gravity and the lock delay, wall kicks, T-Twists, which clears send rows, combos, back-to-back and all clears, bubble rows and cancelling, topping out, hold, the end of each mode, points, saving and the snapshots sent to other players, and that bots take their thinking time over every block), then random play. `node shiba/tests/sim.js [battles] [blocks]` plays each bot level alone, in battles against the level below, and against three person-like players (a beginner, a casual and a practised one), and fails if a level leaves the range of wins it should give them. `node shiba/dev/i18n-check.js` checks that every string exists in both languages and that the brush font has every character it draws. `shiba/dev/` has the Playwright scripts: `smoke.js` (lobby screenshots), `solo.js` (Sprint 40 to the end and its record, Marathon paused, resumed and picked up again after a reload, Two Minutes running out, and a battle against a bot, all with real key presses), `touch.js` (swipes, taps and flicks, then every button in button mode), `online.js` (three browsers and a bot on a local PeerServer: invite link and room code, rounds played with key presses, bubble rows reaching people, chat, a guest reloading mid-round, the next round, back to the room, removing the bot, a win, and the host closing the room) and `layout.js` (the game at nine screen sizes, solo and against three bots, checking that nothing runs off the screen or overlaps), plus `makefont.py` (the brush-font subset) and `artboard.html` (the shiba and the blocks in every mood).

### Adding a game

Put it in its own folder at the top of the repo, named after the game (the folder name becomes the address, like `/Games/poker/`). Give its lobby an "All games" link to `../`, add an entry to the `GAMES` list in `hub/index.html` with its card artwork, and run `python3 hub/build.py`. Each card has a Chinese and an English version of its lettering and artwork, and they don't mix: no English wordmark on the Chinese page, and no Chinese seal or characters on the English one. Use a PeerJS id prefix of its own so its rooms stay separate, and keep the shared `mh_name`, `mh_av` and `mh_lang` settings so players keep their name, avatar and language.
